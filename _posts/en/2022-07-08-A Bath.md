---
title: A Bath
locale: en
---

Over the past few months, I have sometimes taken a bath in the evening. The bathroom light is a little too bright, hard on the eyes. But with it off, the room is too dark to see anything. <!--more-->

Later, I found an unused table lamp. I would switch it on and draw the shower curtain a little way across. That was much more comfortable.

I took a bath tonight too. As I was drying myself with a towel, I suddenly noticed how quiet everything was, how soft the light. For some reason, I thought of the summers I spent as a child at the factory in Guangdong where my parents worked.

It was less a particular scene or picture than a haze of memory dampening the light, somehow bringing with it the familiar, faintly pungent smell of an old air conditioner. A summer holiday was long in those days. The wall-mounted unit would often lose its strength before we were halfway through. A man would arrive on a motorcycle with refrigerant to service it.

Once it was fixed, the room would be properly cold again. I would jump on the bed with all the more energy and end up sweating anyway. I think the bed back home had hard wooden boards, or perhaps it simply was not as much fun to jump on. Whatever the reason, jumping on the bed in Guangdong was a novelty.

Sweating meant having to wash. I remember a long stretch of open air between the bedroom and the bathroom, with very little light. It was frightening for a child. So I would run there, wash as quickly and carelessly as I could, and run back even faster. My feet were still wet, and dust would stick to them as I ran.

I did not jump on the bed every day. Sometimes I watched television with my parents and listened to them talk. Sometimes I danced with my mother, the two of us laughing and making up the steps until we were both tired. On other evenings they were busy, perhaps doing chemical experiments or making products, and came back late. I would read by myself, mostly storybooks. I read *The Little Mermaid* several times, curious and heartsore, almost in tears. I have not read it since. What I vaguely remember is waves, sunlight, and colored foam.

The nights were not always peaceful either. The blanket was not very big, and the bed creaked if I turned over too vigorously. As I drifted off, a family of mice would begin moving about in the space above the ceiling. I did not have back pain then. I always woke the next morning full of energy. Thinking of it now, my parents must have been exhausted. My mother has spoken of the earlier years, when she worked twelve-hour days in an electronics factory and then went out to run a stall at the night market. It really was hard. How could it have been so hard?

By the time I have written this much, my hair has long since dried. I cannot keep writing forever. After a day's work, I am feeling a little homesick. I do not know when, or where, I will make a home. I always feel like a guest from somewhere else, never settled. So I never feel much like buying things for the place where I live.

> So much in this world falls short of what we hoped,
> but life still has to go on.
>
> The sun still rises every day,
> and hope will always live in your heart.
